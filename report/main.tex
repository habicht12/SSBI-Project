\section{Question, approaches and data}
Disease-associated cells may be rare within a blood sample. Arvaniti and Claassen~\cite{cellcnn}
address this problem with CellCNN: shared filters score individual cells, pooling summarizes
their responses, and a classifier learns which summaries predict the sample label. Learning
the representation together with the prediction can focus on informative subsets that
unsupervised clustering might overlook. Citrus\cite{citrus} instead clusters cells first and
selects predictive cluster abundances or marker summaries using a regularized model.
A single-cell SVM assigns each training cell its donor's label and learns a cell-level
decision function. The paper also examines logistic regression, random forests, outlier
detection, moment summaries and a denoising autoencoder. We focus on CellCNN, Citrus
and a linear SVM: how accurately do they predict CMV status, and which cells support
their predictions?

The supplied benchmark\cite{dataset}, derived from Horowitz et al.\cite{horowitz}, contains
\textbf{20 donors: 11 CMV-negative and 9 CMV-positive}. We use \texttt{gated\_alive}
(3,438,750 cells), which retains live, singlet peripheral blood mononuclear cells without
an additional NK gate, matching the paper's broad-cell analysis. The smaller
\texttt{gated\_NK} files served only for technical tests. We retain all 37 supplied analysis
markers, as in the official example; the paper describes 36. Technical channels are excluded.

All analysis-marker values are finite and the marker schema is consistent. We apply
$\operatorname{arcsinh}(x/5)$ to compress the intensity range while retaining negative
background values. No further gating or bead normalization is introduced: the files are
already gated and bead reference data are unavailable. Measurement-day patterns remain
a possible technical influence. For exploration, we sample 500 cells per donor without
replacement (seed $42+i$ for donor $i$ in sorted order), giving each donor equal weight.
Marker-wise standardization uses this 10,000-cell sample. This joint exploratory fit is
kept separate from classifier preprocessing, which uses training donors only.

\section{Dimensions, parameters and neighbourhoods}
We compare eight two-dimensional projections of these same standardized 37-marker
profiles: PCA with and without whitening; t-SNE\cite{tsne} with perplexity 5, 30 and 50;
and UMAP\cite{umap} with neighbour count/minimum-distance pairs (15, 0.1), (50, 0.1)
and (15, 0.5).
Whitening rescales PCA axes; perplexity and UMAP neighbour count control neighbourhood
size, while \texttt{min\_dist} controls how tightly UMAP points can pack. We fix two
components, Euclidean distances and seed 42; t-SNE uses PCA initialization and 1,000
iterations, UMAP spectral initialization and 500 epochs. Other tunable choices include
initialization, learning rate, iteration count and distance metric.

To compare maps despite arbitrary orientations, we use local neighbour overlap rather
than coordinate differences. For cell $i$ and maps $A,B$, let $N_{15}^{A}(i)$ be its 15
nearest other cells. We calculate
\[
  J_i(A,B)=\frac{|N_{15}^{A}(i)\cap N_{15}^{B}(i)|}
                    {|N_{15}^{A}(i)\cup N_{15}^{B}(i)|}.
\]
We average first within donors and then across donors. Values range from 0 (no shared
neighbours) to 1 (identical sets). All 28 map pairs are evaluated; comparison with the
standardized 37D space additionally measures neighbourhood retention. This is a local
geometric criterion, not evidence that visible islands are biological cell types.

Perplexity 5 fragments the t-SNE map more strongly than 30 (Fig.~\ref{fig:projections}).
The highest 37D retention within each method is 0.0102 for unwhitened PCA,
0.1824 for t-SNE at 30 and 0.0917 for UMAP at (15, 0.1).
PCA whitening makes a negligible difference. Increasing UMAP neighbours or
\texttt{min\_dist} lowers retention to 0.0738 or 0.0531, respectively.
We therefore use t-SNE at 30 for interpretation. This choice concerns local geometry
within our small grid; distances between islands and their areas are not biological
distances or population frequencies.

\section{Clustering and biological plausibility}
We cluster the same 10,000 cells in standardized \textbf{37D space}. K-Means minimizes
within-cluster squared distances; Ward merges groups with the smallest increase in this
quantity. Both use 6, 10 or 14 clusters (K-Means: 20 initializations, seed 42).
Leiden\cite{leiden} finds communities on an unweighted, undirected 15-neighbour union
graph; we test resolutions 0.5, 1.0 and 1.5 with the configuration-model objective and seed 42.

Selection maximizes donor-averaged silhouette, comparing within-cluster distances with
distances to the nearest other cluster. All nine solutions use the same 100 evaluation cells
per donor (2,000 total; seed $43+i$), approximating full-sample distances to limit computation.

K-Means with six clusters has the highest silhouette (0.079), ahead of Leiden with 11
(0.067) and Ward with 14 (0.049). The low values indicate substantial overlap.
CD3/CD4 or CD3/CD8 support T-compatible groups, CD19 supports B-compatible groups,
and CD56/CD16/CD94 support NK-compatible groups\cite{horowitz}.
K-Means provides the clearest coarse summary. Leiden separates a more distinct
CD19-rich group and additional heterogeneous profiles, making it a useful finer-grained
description. Ward adds subdivisions with several mixed profiles.
\textbf{No biological winner is established}: silhouette favours compact geometry, and
manual cell-type reference labels are unavailable.

\section{Donor-level classification benchmark}
We use the same 100 Monte Carlo splits for all methods, following the paper: each split
trains on seven donors per CMV group and tests on the remaining four negative and two
positive donors. The 14 training donors are divided into three fixed inner folds for model
selection. Cells from a donor never cross a train--validation--test boundary. The saved
split seeds determine sampling; learned scaling, clustering and feature selection are
restricted to the relevant training fold.

\textbf{CellCNN.} Our Python 3.12/PyTorch implementation applies linear filters, ReLU,
mean pooling of the strongest 1\% of responses per filter, and a two-class softmax output.
Each inner training donor supplies 200 inputs of 3,000 cells sampled with replacement,
giving donors equal weight and emphasizing rare high-response cells. A training-only
scaler uses 20,000 cells per donor. We evaluate 3, 4 and 5 filters in each inner fold
(nine candidates), with Adam, learning rate 0.01, batch size 128, L2 penalty $10^{-4}$,
no dropout, and at most 100 epochs. Early stopping uses validation loss with patience 5.
The candidate with highest donor-level validation accuracy is retained; ties use ROC-AUC,
then validation loss, then fewer filters and fold order. As in the paper, this selected
network remains trained on its nine or ten inner training donors; it is not refitted on 14.

We fix this small filter grid instead of repeating the paper's broader random search.
For validation and test prediction we average five softmax probabilities, each from a
random input of up to 20,000 cells sampled without replacement. The paper's NK benchmark
uses one test input; our averaging reduces sampling variation. Donor-level candidate
evaluation also differs from evaluation of individual generated inputs in the reference.

\textbf{Citrus.} We use the original R implementation (v0.8; pinned source revisions).
Each donor supplies 1,000 randomly sampled cells, limiting the cost of hierarchical
clustering. Clustering uses arcsinh-transformed markers without an additional marker
z-score. Clusters containing at least 5\% of training events supply donor-level abundance
features to L1-logistic regression, with feature standardization inside the training fit.
The regularization parameter minimizes inner classification error; ties favour stronger
regularization. Clustering and regression are then fitted on all 14 training donors.
Test cells are mapped through their nearest training cell into the fixed hierarchy.
We assemble the original fold-clustering functions around our shared donor splits,
preserving Citrus's clustering, feature calculation and regression.

\textbf{Linear SVM.} We sample 10,000 cells per training donor without replacement,
standardize using training cells, and attach each donor's label to its cells. An L2-penalized
linear SVM with squared-hinge loss is selected from $C\in\{0.01,0.1,1\}$ by mean inner
ROC-AUC (ties favour smaller $C$), then refitted on all 14 donors. Its donor score is the
mean of the highest 1\% of cell margins, evaluated on all cells of a test donor. This fixed
aggregation targets rare informative cells. Balanced sampling and donor aggregation are
our explicit adaptation of the paper's cell-level baseline. The decision threshold maximizes
sensitivity plus specificity on inner out-of-fold donor scores.

ROC-AUC is the primary metric: it measures how often a positive donor ranks above a
negative one. Average precision (AP) summarizes precision as recall increases;
balanced accuracy averages sensitivity and specificity at the chosen threshold.
CellCNN and Citrus use 0.5, whereas the SVM uses its inner-validated threshold.
CellCNN and SVM both attain median ROC-AUC 0.875; the median paired difference is
0.000. CellCNN exceeds Citrus by a median paired 0.250 and has higher median balanced
accuracy than SVM. These results support useful discrimination, but do not establish
CellCNN superiority over SVM in ranking donors.

\section{Which cells support the predictions?}
We reuse the saved models and the t-SNE map with perplexity 30. A cell is scored only
by models for which its donor was an \textbf{outer test donor}, using the original training
scaler. For cell $i$ from donor $d$, let $S_d$ be the set of those test splits. Its positive
selection frequency is
\[
 f_i^+=\frac{1}{|S_d|}\sum_{s\in S_d}
 \mathbf{1}\{i\text{ is positively selected by model }s\}.
\]
The indicator is 1 when selected and 0 otherwise. The denominator includes all eligible
models, including null models, and varies between donors. Negative selection is defined
analogously; Fig.~\ref{fig:interpretation} focuses on positive selection.

For \textbf{CellCNN}, a cell is selected if a filter response exceeds half that filter's
maximum over all cells of its inner training donors. Selection is positive when the
filter's output weight for CMV-positive exceeds its weight for CMV-negative.
This paper-inspired phenotype rule differs from membership in the top-1\% pooling
subset; a cell can be selected in both directions by different filters.
For \textbf{Citrus}, we sum the regression coefficients of all hierarchical clusters to
which the cell maps. A positive sum above $10^{-10}$ defines positive selection;
coefficients below that absolute tolerance are treated as zero. For \textbf{SVM}, we select
exactly the highest $\lceil0.01N_d\rceil$ margins among all $N_d$ cells of the test donor,
breaking ties by event index. Selected cells above the learned donor threshold count as
positive; those below it count as negative.

At least one eligible model positively selects 1,313 map cells for CellCNN, 7,678 for
Citrus and 219 for SVM. The SVM selects a small subset with elevated CD56, NKG2C and
CD57 relative to the map reference; CellCNN shows the same marker trends less strongly
for NKG2C/CD57. Both profiles retain high CD3. They therefore support NK-associated
receptor patterns with T-compatible or heterogeneous contributions, not an isolated
CD3-negative NK population. The positive Citrus profile is CD3/CD8-rich, with low CD56
and NKG2C. Its broad coverage and mostly low frequencies do not define a narrow,
consistently selected population.

These summaries describe the 10,000 map cells. For SVM, a donor's positive profile may
rest on just one mapped cell. Stronger marker enrichment consequently does not establish
better biological recovery. The paper groups filter-subset centroids across runs; our
cell-wise held-out frequencies provide a different view and do not reproduce its
centroid groups or their reported selection rates.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
The main result is comparable median ranking performance for CellCNN and linear SVM,
with lower performance for Citrus under the chosen sampling and 5\% cluster-size threshold.
These choices may affect sensitivity to rare populations. Fifteen Citrus null models remain
in all summaries. Only 20 donors are independent; the overlapping splits are not independent
studies. Both CMV groups occur on every measurement day, but batch effects remain possible.
Different hardware and concurrent execution preclude a fair runtime comparison:
CellCNN used a GPU, SVM and Citrus CPUs.

Saved interpretation checks reconstruct all 600 SVM and CellCNN donor scores per method;
Citrus checks relative logits because its intercept was not exported. These arithmetic checks
support consistency, not biological validation. Full settings, seeds, provenance and
supplementary plots remain in the accompanying notebooks and local analysis exports.
