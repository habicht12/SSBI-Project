\section{Reference study, dataset and preprocessing}
\subsection{The reference study and the implemented comparison}
CellCNN learns a sample representation from labelled bags of cells~\cite{cellcnn}. Its shared filters can respond to uncommon marker combinations; pooling prevents a small informative subset from being averaged indiscriminately into the entire sample. The paper studies several settings, including stimulated immune cells, survival-associated subsets, CMV-associated NK populations and artificial leukaemia spike-ins. These experiments have different endpoints and should not be treated as a single CMV benchmark. Our project evaluates donor CMV classification and subset interpretation in the supplied cohort.

The original CellCNN implementation used Theano/Keras. Our implementation reproduces the relevant linear-filter/ReLU/pooling design in PyTorch, with explicitly recorded adaptations (S3). Citrus is the original R package~\cite{citrus}. Published results are background evidence; all numerical results in the main report come from this project's current analyses.

\subsection{Analysis scopes and donor balance}
The supplied archive derives from the study of NK-cell diversity by Horowitz et al.~\cite{horowitz}; the supplied 20-donor subset is smaller than the publication's complete cohort. The archive provides NK-gated cells and live-cell PBMC files for the same donors. Labels belong to donors and do not identify infected individual cells. Four complete twin pairs are identifiable from the supplied donor identities; related donors can therefore remain on opposite sides of a donor-wise split.

\subsection{Transformation and scaling}
We apply $a_{ij}=\arcsinh(x_{ij}/5)$ to each analysis marker. This compresses large intensities while retaining finite negative background measurements. The selected cofactor is fixed, not optimised by outer test performance. Exploratory maps and clustering use marker scaling fitted to their own balanced samples. CellCNN and SVM use a scaler fitted only on the relevant training donors. Citrus clusters unstandardised ArcSinh marker values; its logistic regression standardises cluster-frequency features internally.

The classification implementations preserve their original numerical paths: CellCNN/SVM convert raw values to float32 before ArcSinh, whereas Citrus uses the native float64 path. Marker means are accumulated in float64. For the new Citrus interpretation, all 127,233,750 raw marker values across 3,438,750 events were independently compared between FlowKit and native Citrus/FlowCore and agreed byte for byte.

\section{Dimensionality reduction and clustering}
\subsection{Projection parameters and comparison measures}
PCA, t-SNE and UMAP use the same 40,000-cell NK sample. PCA fits 36 components; the first 29 retain 91.4978\% of variance and define the high-dimensional reference for the nonlinear methods and quality scores. The first two retain 20.2911\%. Retaining variance is not equivalent to proving removal of measurement noise.

t-SNE uses PCA initialisation, Barnes--Hut optimisation, 750 iterations and seed 42. Its six perplexities are 5, 30, 50, 60, 70 and 100. UMAP tests neighbours $\{5,15,50,100\}$, minimum distance $\{0,0.1,0.5,1\}$ and learning rate $\{0.1,0.5,1,5\}$, with seed 42 and otherwise the recorded library defaults. The sweep uses a fixed 5,000-cell subset of the selected PC scores. Additional tunable choices include PCA whitening/solver, t-SNE learning rate/initialisation and the metric, initialisation and iteration settings of UMAP; they are not additional experimental factors in this analysis.

For neighbourhood size $k=15$, trustworthiness is
\[
T(k)=1-\frac{2}{nk(2n-3k-1)}\sum_i\sum_{j\in U_k(i)}\bigl(r(i,j)-k\bigr),
\]
where $U_k(i)$ contains low-dimensional neighbours absent from the high-dimensional neighbourhood and $r(i,j)$ is their high-dimensional rank~\cite{trust}. False neighbours are penalised more when originally distant. The final 40,000-cell calculation uses exact ranks in row blocks. kNN preservation is the mean fraction of shared neighbours, $|N_k^A(i)\cap N_k^B(i)|/k$; it gives no partial credit to near misses. Distance correlation is Pearson correlation of pairwise distances within one fixed 2,000-cell subset.

For Procrustes comparison, corresponding cells are aligned by centring, rotation/reflection and scaling; the sum of squared residuals is the reported disparity $M^2$~\cite{scipy}. It evaluates aligned global shape, whereas neighbourhood overlap evaluates local agreement. Both are geometric comparisons, not biological validation.

\subsection{Clustering grid and stability}
The clustering sample is drawn by one NumPy generator with seed 42, advanced sequentially in the supplied donor-file order. Each donor contributes 1,000 finite-marker events. K-means uses ten initialisations for $k\in\{2,3,4,6,8,10,12\}$. Hierarchical clustering evaluates the same cuts for single, average, complete and Ward linkage. Leiden uses a weighted undirected neighbour graph constructed with Scanpy, neighbour counts $\{10,30,60\}$, resolutions $\{0.3,0.7,1.2\}$, the \texttt{leidenalg} backend and convergence iterations~\cite{leiden}. All fits and DB scores use standardised 37D data.

DB gives each cluster equal weight:
\[
DB=\frac1K\sum_c\max_{j\ne c}\frac{S_c+S_j}{\|\mu_c-\mu_j\|},
\]
where $S_c$ is mean distance to centroid $\mu_c$~\cite{db}. This differs from a cell-weighted mean silhouette, but does not protect against small artefactual groups. Single, average and complete linkage select partitions dominated by one giant cluster; all are retained in the complete results rather than silently removed.

For each of ten repeats, 80\% of the initially sampled cells are retained within each donor. Scaling and clustering are refitted. On retained cells, each original cluster takes its maximum Jaccard match to a new cluster. We first take a median across assessed repeats for each cluster, then an unweighted mean over clusters. An absent cluster is unassessable in that repeat; a singleton may score one whenever retained. The stability cutoff 0.6 is heuristic. This procedure tests cell removal and algorithmic/scaling changes within the original sample, not generalisation to new donors.

\section{Classification and subset implementation}
\subsection{Shared splits and model selection}
Splits 0--29 are the common three-method benchmark; CellCNN/SVM also have 100 stored splits. Every outer training set has 14 donors (7 CMV-positive, 7 negative), and each test set has two positive and four negative donors. Three fixed inner folds are shared across methods. These are repeated holdouts of the same 20 people, not 30 independent studies. All scaler fits and training inputs exclude outer test donors.

CellCNN uses Adam with learning rate 0.01, batch size 128, L2 coefficient $10^{-4}$, at most 100 epochs and patience five. The validation-loss improvement tolerance is $10^{-6}$. No dropout is used. Inner training and validation bags are materialised with separate seeds. The retained network was trained on nine or ten inner training donors; it is not refitted on all 14. Validation and test average five probabilities from up to 20,000 sampled cells per input. A fixed 3/4/5-filter search replaces the publication's random search; averaging multiple test inputs is another adaptation.

For Citrus, final training uses 140,000 cells. Its original mapping attaches a new cell to the nearest training event, then transfers that event's memberships in the nested hierarchy. Regression operates on cluster frequencies, not cell labels. The minimum cluster size is 70 events in the final training set. The existing lambda path is constructed using the full outer training set, while inner trees and fitted weights use the specified training folds. Thirty splits and 10,000 cells/donor are computational reductions relative to the larger paper protocol.

For SVM, $m_i=w^Tz_i+b$ is a margin, not a probability. With $I_d$ the highest-scoring $k_d=\lceil0.01N_d\rceil$ cells, $s_d=k_d^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_d}m_i$. The threshold maximises sensitivity plus specificity on inner out-of-fold donor scores. CellCNN/Citrus use threshold 0.5; SVM retains its inner-trained threshold. AUC and AP use scores, whereas BA uses the declared thresholded decisions. AP summarises precision at positive-recall increments; it is not an alternative name for trapezoidal PR area.

\subsection{Quantitative cell-subset scores}
For cell $i$, split $s$, filter $f$ and cluster $j$, the positive-direction scores are
\[
\begin{aligned}
q^{\mathrm{CNN}}_{is}&=\max_{f:\,\Delta_{fs}>0,\,M_{fs}>0}r_{ifs}/M_{fs}, &q^{\mathrm{CNN}}_{is}&>0.5,\\
q^{\mathrm{SVM}}_{is}&=m_{is}-\tau_s\quad(i\in I_{ds}), &q^{\mathrm{SVM}}_{is}&>0,\\
q^{\mathrm{Citrus}}_{is}&=\max(\{0\}\cup\{\beta_{js}:i\in A_{js}\}), &q^{\mathrm{Citrus}}_{is}&>10^{-10}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $\Delta_{fs}=v_{f,+}-v_{f,-}$ is the output-weight contrast. CellCNN's $M_{fs}$ is the maximum response on the original scaler-reference draw: 20,000 cells per actual inner training donor with the original candidate seed. It is never recomputed from test or display cells. A filter with zero reference maximum is ineligible; an empty maximum is defined as zero. New cells may exceed the reference maximum. SVM cells outside $I_{ds}$ are unselected; boundary ties use the original FCS event index. Citrus's $A_{js}$ denotes transferred training-cluster membership, and $10^{-10}$ excludes numerical zeros.

The plotted cell frequency is
\[
f_i=\frac{\sum_{s\in\mathcal T_d}\mathbf1\{\text{cell }i\text{ passes the method's selection rule in }s\}}
{|\mathcal T_d|},
\]
where $\mathcal T_d$ contains all outer test appearances of its donor. Each cell counts at most once per split even if several filters or nested clusters select it. All methods use identical denominators of 4--16 visits. In Citrus, splits 19, 24 and 26 contain no effective clusters; split 27 contains only negative coefficients. All four therefore contribute zero positive selections and remain in the denominator.

The display reference is the original 10,000-cell live-PBMC t-SNE map at perplexity 30, with its stored exploratory scaler. It is distinct from the NK-gated Task-2 map. Marker profiles use selected \emph{full-donor} cells: mean cells per nonempty visit, mean visits per donor, then equal means over contributing donors. A visit without selected cells has no marker profile, rather than a vector of zeros. Both CMV classes contribute; all methods have 20 contributing donors. The common display transformation is $(c_j-\mu_j)/\sigma_j$, with $\mu_j,\sigma_j$ from the reference map, not model weights.

\subsection{Reconstruction and numerical checks}
The original Citrus exports retained coefficients and cluster means but not tree memberships. We reconstructed only the original final Ward trees with their original donor draws and seeds, retaining the selected classifiers. Every saved cluster profile in a reconstructed tree agrees within $5.33\times10^{-15}$. Full test-donor cells are then mapped to these trees; original 10,000-cell benchmark predictions are not replaced by new full-donor predictions.

Exact nearest-training-cell selection is accelerated by proving nonselection when an actual point outside the positive cluster union is closer than the exact nearest positive member. An approximate query supplies only possible negative witnesses; unresolved cases use an exact full search, with explicit first-row tie handling. ``Negative'' here means outside the selected training union, not a CMV-negative donor. Across 26 models with positive selections, 9,984 native Citrus control queries match nearest indices and decisions exactly. Caches bind data, code, tree files and numerical package versions. Separate numerical checks also recomputed donor metrics, aggregation and additional exhaustive nearest-neighbour cases. These checks establish implementation consistency, not biological validity.

\section{Modified CellCNN: detailed methods}
\subsection{Common protocol and linear baseline}
All four models use the same 100 outer donor splits and the same three-fold/filter-count candidate design. The baseline is the original linear/ReLU CellCNN. Training bag size, sampling, Adam settings, filter counts, early stopping and five-input test averaging remain as in S3. For the baseline and hard quadratic model, the per-filter pooled response is the mean of the largest $\max(1,\lfloor0.01N\rfloor)$ responses. This floor convention differs from SVM's ceiling convention; both are retained faithfully.

\subsection{Diagonal quadratic responses}
For standardised cell vector $z$, filter $f$ computes
\[
r_f(z)=\max\left(0,b_f+w_f^Tz+q_f^Tz^{\odot2}\right).
\]
There are no cross-marker products $z_jz_\ell$ for $j\ne\ell$. Positive or negative squared-marker coefficients allow non-monotone responses in individual markers. The new quadratic weights start at zero; the hard-pooling model therefore starts with the same linear response and random initialisation sequence as the baseline. Its loss is cross-entropy plus $10^{-4}$ times the squared linear, quadratic and output weight matrices; biases are not included in that penalty.

\subsection{Learnable soft-fraction pooling}
Sort responses within a bag in descending order, $r_{(j)f}$, and assign midpoint ranks $u_j=(j-\tfrac12)/N$, $j=1,\ldots,N$. The learnt fraction and pooling weights are
\[
\alpha_f=\operatorname{sigmoid}(\theta_f),\qquad
g_{jf}=\operatorname{sigmoid}\left(\frac{\alpha_f-u_j}{0.002}\right),\qquad
h_f=\frac{\sum_jg_{jf}r_{(j)f}}{\max(\sum_jg_{jf},10^{-6})}.
\]
Each filter starts at $\alpha_f=0.01$. The fraction is bounded in $(0,1)$ and trained jointly with the classifier. This is a smooth weighting of sorted responses, not a binary selection mask, and is not exactly hard top-1\% at its initial finite temperature. The quadratic soft model uses the same diagonal response and weight penalties as the hard quadratic model.

\subsection{Mahalanobis/ReLU prototype variant}
The prototype response is
\[
d_f(z)=\max\left(0,\frac1{37}\sum_{j=1}^{37}a_{fj}(z_j-c_{fj})^2\right),
\qquad r_f(z)=\max(0,\rho_f-d_f(z)).
\]
Positive weights are parameterised as $\tilde a_{fj}=\operatorname{softplus}(u_{fj})+10^{-6}$ and normalised to mean one within each filter. Radii use $\rho_f=\operatorname{softplus}(v_f)+10^{-6}$. Thus the metric is diagonal; a full covariance matrix is not learned. Soft pooling is defined as above.

Initial centres are sampled from a donor-balanced training-only pool of 2,000 cells per donor, using a separate seed offset of 300,000. Weights start equally, and each initial radius is the 1\% quantile of its prototype distances, bounded below by $2\times10^{-6}$. Training adds output-weight L2 ($10^{-4}$) and a geometry penalty $10^{-3}\operatorname{mean}[(a-1)^2]$; centres, radii and geometry are not given the quadratic model's weight penalty. Validation loss contains cross-entropy and output L2 but excludes the geometry penalty. Consequently this is an exploratory architecture comparison, not an isolated test of response geometry with every regulariser held identical.

\subsection{Separate population-frequency evaluation}
Bonus frequency AUC uses one filter per model and split: the filter with the largest positive output contrast. Its half-maximum threshold comes from the actual inner-training reference. Evaluation uses a fixed 20,000-cell sample per donor (seed 63,000 plus sorted donor index). This is distinct from the union-of-positive-filters definition used in Task 5.

The baseline lacks a positive filter in splits 44 and 84. Those frequencies remain missing, not zero. Population-frequency comparisons use the same 98 jointly evaluable splits for all four models, whereas network-score comparisons use all 100. Frequency effect means positive-donor frequency minus negative-donor frequency within a split; it is not a causal disease effect.

These different summaries show why performance of the whole learned representation and performance of a single selected population must be separated.
